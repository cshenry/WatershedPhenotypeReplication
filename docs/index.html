The answer

The deliverable compares four datasets over the same 519 genomes and 324 Biolog conditions: the collaborators' original 2022 output, our resimulation of those same 2022 models, the 2025 KBase re-annotated models, and a full 2026 rebuild from the original genomes. The question is replication fidelity — how much of the 2022 result survives each change.

Current code reproduces ~90% of the 2022 calls.

Resimulating the original 2022 models with today's pipeline matches the collaborators' own growth/no-growth calls on 90% of conditions. Re-annotating the genomes (2025k) and rebuilding the models from scratch (2026p) each shift only about 10% of growth conditions relative to that 2022 baseline — and all three resimulated sets predict the real experimental Biolog data about equally well.

The four datasets

One baseline and three resimulations, each run through the identical downstream pipeline — gap-fill on pyruvate, simulate the 324-condition Biolog panel, call growth at the same threshold — so any difference measures the models and annotations, not the treatment.

Accuracy against the real Biolog data

Replication fidelity says whether we reproduced the 2022 run; this says whether the predictions are actually right, scored against the measured K-12 Biolog growth calls. This is the referee for model quality, secondary to fidelity but the metric that ultimately matters.

A methodological finding: the models can fix CO₂ — and shouldn't

Worth knowing before publishing predictions built on these models. It is not specific to this study: the defect is in the reconstruction template, so it reaches any model built from it.

E. coli models arrive able to grow on CO₂ with no organic carbon

Freshly gap-filled models can build biomass from CO₂ + minerals — no sugar, no organic carbon at all — and the capability is identical across every genome, the signature of a template-level defect rather than anything genome-specific. E. coli is not an autotroph.

The cause is reaction directionality: two reactions are declared reversible that are one-way in life, and the solver runs them backwards to manufacture reducing power, which then drives CO₂ fixation.

  • rxn05759 (hydrogen:ferredoxin oxidoreductase) — charge-imbalanced by 2; run in reverse it creates free electrons.
  • rxn03978 (assimilatory nitrite reductase) — run forwards it becomes NH₃ → NO₂⁻, i.e. ammonia oxidation yielding reductant. Ammonia is the confirmed energy source: remove NH₃ from the medium and the autotrophy disappears entirely.

It slips past both standard guardrails: ATP correction doesn't see it (the leak is electrons, not ATP) and mass-balance checks pass it (the formulas balance — only the charge is wrong).

What we do about it — and why these results are clean

Both reactions are constrained to their physiological direction before gap-filling, and CO₂ uptake is blocked at simulation time — restoring a bound the Biolog media already declare. Since the Biolog media supply no CO₂, the artifact cannot affect the growth predictions reported here. With that fix plus ATP-safe gap-filling, every model in all four datasets is free of the fatal "free-lunch" failure (biomass from minerals alone) — 0 free-lunch models across —. A residual autotrophy persists on some models via a further ammonia-oxidation route we did not chase; it is a model-quality issue for the ModelSEED developers, not a threat to these results.

Methods — the reconstructed 2022 pipeline

The method is not inferred. It is the collaborators' own 2022 notebook, run with current code.

  1. Gap-fill each model on Carbon-Pyruvic-Acid, ATP-safe, with the leaky-reversible reactions constrained first. The saved 2022 models are auxotrophic — without this they predict zero growth on everything, glucose included.
  2. Simulate the ecoli_biolog phenotype set (324 Biolog conditions) with missing transporters auto-added and CO₂ uptake blocked.
  3. Score the growth/no-growth calls two ways: against the collaborators' own 2022 output (replication fidelity), and against the experimental Biolog data (accuracy).
  4. Verify every gap-filled model actually grows before it is scored — a model that fails to grow on its own gap-fill medium is flagged, not silently reported.

A trap worth flagging: a model's vintage is not in its name

The narrative workspace holds both 2022 and 2025 models; some 2025 models are named plainly, and one genome has two objects with the identical name distinguishable only by save date. Selecting “the latest version” silently grabs the 2025 model and labels it 2022. This pipeline splits by genome-set marker and save date; all 519 genomes carry a genuine 2022 model recovered from object history.
